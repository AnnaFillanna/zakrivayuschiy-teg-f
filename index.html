Sprint 1: A Blank Slate

</HTML>

At the beginning, we worked through the fears and doubts that many beginners experience. One of them is the fear of a blank page. It is much more than being afraid of an empty screen. Behind it are often deeper questions: Where should I start? What if it becomes too difficult? What if I cannot handle it?

Moving to another country taught me how to start over. There was a lot of uncertainty, many mistakes, and plenty of doubts, but every new experience became part of my story and made me stronger.

Sprint 1: What If I Fail?

</HTML>

The first project was complete, but the journey had only just begun. The excitement of success could quickly turn into fear of failure. Or, on the contrary, that first achievement could become the motivation to keep going and believe in myself.

Over time, life became brighter. New ideas appeared, along with my own brand, first projects, and dreams that no longer seemed impossible. Sometimes, the most important thing is simply not to stop.

Sprint 2: Chasing Perfection

</HTML>

By this stage, I already knew enough about layout and styling to understand how much there was still to learn. Progress is always a moving target, so instead of trying to make everything perfect, I learned to focus on building, experimenting, and improving.

There were moments when I wanted to give up. But those challenges helped me understand what I truly wanted. The contrast between the past and the present reminds me how far I have already come.

Sprint 2: The People Around You

</HTML>

Throughout this journey, I was never completely alone, even when it sometimes felt that way. Fellow students, mentors, and the people closest to me were there along the way. Learning something new becomes easier when there are people around you who understand and support your goals.

Not every day feels inspiring. Sometimes progress is almost invisible. But even the smallest steps can grow into meaningful changes over time.

Sprint 3: Finding Another Way

</HTML>

During the course, I constantly worked on different challenges. At some point, it could feel as though all possible solutions had disappeared. That was usually the moment to step back and look at the problem from a different perspective.

When I started looking at familiar things differently, new possibilities appeared. What once seemed like a problem became experience, and things I used to be afraid of gradually turned into skills.

Sprint 3: When Motivation Fades

</HTML>

While studying, there are moments when it feels impossible to decide what to focus on first. There is a project to finish, exercises to solve, theory to understand, and everyday life still happening at the same time. In moments like these, focus becomes especially important.

I still cannot see the entire future clearly. Sometimes it feels blurred and uncertain. But I already know one thing: even when the path is not completely clear, moving forward is still worth it.
